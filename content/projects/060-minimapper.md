MiniMapper is a tool for real-time generative videomapping. Each surface is a deformable Bezier patch that takes its texture from Hydra graphics, a p5 sketch, video, images, the camera, or another window on the computer. It runs in the browser, with nothing to install. Sessions can be saved at any time.

This project grew out of the friction of installing and using commercial mapping software. MiniMapper was built on two scales: decisions that set the overall direction of the tool, and specific directions that were tested with students in a lab.

From August to November 2026 the lab used it for the whole semester, and the code went from 2,473 to 6,355 lines. Almost all of it came from what the lab's teams asked for. A Giphy link that wouldn't load led to a link field that translates whatever the share button gives you. The camera, hands, silhouette and microphone now give readings that can be used from code, and the canvas can be split across two projectors, with each one's color adjusted separately.

The aim was to close the distance between software that operates as a black box and small, customizable programs: the ones that cut down on technical complications and learning curves, and keep the focus on creative decisions.
